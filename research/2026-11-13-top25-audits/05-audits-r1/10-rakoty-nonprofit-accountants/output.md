# Opportunity Audit — Rakoty Nonprofit Accountants

Your site sets out a clear nonprofit practice: bookkeeping, grant reporting, audit leadership and 990s, with about two dozen client logos. Each page we opened on 13 November 2026 sat behind a loader stuck at 9–32%, with nothing behind it.

## 1. Repair the front door

**Now:** the header "Schedule an appointment" button has no link and the in-page one lands on a "not found" page; with no form, enquiries come by phone or email.

**Would do:** it fixes the loader and speed, restores booking, adds a short form and tap-to-call, publishes address and hours, then drafts each reply and call slot for a person to send.

**Impact:** visitors can read the offer and ask for a call: on our assumption of 2–6 enquiries a month, 1–2 converting at your published $99/month = about $1,180–$2,370 a year.

**Pilot:** 2–3 weeks, with before-and-after phone screenshots.

## 2. Chase documents and deadlines

**Now:** your nonprofit page lists monthly close by the 10th, monthly budget-to-actual plus monthly and quarterly grant reports, the annual audit, 990s and 1099/W-2 season. Worth checking together: does each start with someone emailing a client for a missing statement?

**Would do:** it builds a request list per engagement, sends specific asks, chases on a fixed cadence, files what arrives and lists what is missing.

**Impact:** about 86–345 staff hours a year (24 clients x 1–3 hours chasing per close x 12 closes x 30–40% automated, our assumption) at $35/hour (bookkeeping clerk median, NJ, 2025, x1.25 for benefits) = about $3,010–$12,075 a year.

**Pilot:** 3–4 weeks on 3–5 volunteer clients.

## 3. A grant and 990 answer desk

**Now:** training and support is a listed service and your blog already covers 990s, cost allocation and accrual basis. Worth checking together: how much of a week goes to repeating those answers?

**Would do:** it answers from your articles, checklists and client agreements with citations, sends judgement calls to the Client Manager, and keeps client documents separate.

**Impact:** about 43–172 staff hours a year (24 clients x 0.5–1.5 hours of routine questions per client per month x 12 months x 30–40% automated, our assumption) at $60/hour (accountant median, NJ, 2025, x1.25 for benefits) = about $2,580–$10,320 a year.

**Pilot:** 3–4 weeks, scored on 30 real past questions.

## Why this works

- A person approves each outbound reply and submission.
- It sits beside WordPress, Tag Manager, QuickBooks and email.
- Client tax data stays in your systems.
