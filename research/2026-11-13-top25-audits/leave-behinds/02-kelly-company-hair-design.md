# Opportunity Audit — Kelly & Company Hair Design

Nine services from haircuts to lashes, a 4.8-star rating, an August 2026 rebuild — and a site thinner than the business.

### 1. Make the front door readable again

**Now (checked 13 Nov 2026):** 11 of 11 booking-page images fail, five footer links are dead, the home page has no title, a "Hello world!" post is live, and Google still says "Claim this business".

**Agent:** real page titles, markup assistants can quote, logo and links repaired, placeholder deleted, an owner-approved answer block, weekly Google checks.

**Pilot (2 weeks):** before-and-after of what Maps and an assistant answer.

### 2. Answer the phone when you are closed

**Now:** two ways in — the phone and a callback-only request form; no prices or live availability published; closed Sunday and Monday. Worth checking together: how many calls arrive then?

**Agent:** voice, text and web intake on your number, answering only from an owner-approved sheet, otherwise taking a callback number; bookings confirmed; up-dos, colour corrections and complaints go to a person.

**Pilot (3 weeks):** one tracked number; calls, questions, bookings logged.

### 3. Keep the notes from the chair

**Now:** the services page says every hair treatment starts with a hair analysis, and the page source shows no client-record tool; the one visible complaint is colour and length, not billing.

**Agent:** three staff-only fields after each appointment (formula, service, preferences) and a stylist-approved message before colour and up-do bookings; health questions to a person.

**Pilot (3–4 weeks):** 60 days of client cards, plus colour and up-do consults.

## Why this works

- Autonomy: it drafts; nothing goes live without your approval.
- Integration: your WordPress site, phone and Google listing stay put.
- Privacy: you approve prices, hours and messages; chair notes stay staff-only.

## Suggested first step

A 20-minute walkthrough, then a small paid pilot.
