Opportunity Audit — Amertech Engineering, Inc.

You have engineered land in central New Jersey for decades — your history page counts over 80 years. But a developer meets a site whose email links are placeholders.

1. Make the front door work on a phone

Today (checked 13 Nov 2026): the header email link and the contact page's email text both point at placeholders, the phone number isn't tappable, and the footer reads "© 2011 – 2016".

What the agent would do: rebuild six pages for a phone, fix the email links, publish hours, add a form that acknowledges enquiries and takes plan uploads.

Pilot: two weeks, before/after on a phone.

2. Sort developer enquiries before an engineer sees them

Today: your form asks name, company, mailing address and "product/service" — no parcel, town or deadline. A 2026 Professional Civil Engineer posting, now closed, listed preparing proposals.

What the agent would do: read each enquiry, pull town, parcel, project type and date, reply with a ticket number and the gaps, book a call.

Pilot: three weeks, 15–25 live enquiries, PE-approved replies.

3. Draft review letters from each town's own rules

Today: your About page lists ten towns; the two files we could open show rounds repeating — Raritan site plans revised April 2025, exhibits revised August 2025, and a July 2025 Old Bridge Community Impact Statement citing Amertech's preliminary plat.

What the agent would do: index each town's ordinance and prior letters; on a resubmission, draft a completeness check, ordinance-cited comments and the earlier points addressed; your engineer edits and seals.

Pilot: four weeks, one town, one live application.

Why this works

  • Nothing to rip out. A form, a mailbox and a folder are enough.
  • A person stays in charge. Your engineer approves each fee and reply.
  • Your documents stay yours. Plans sit in a private index.

Suggested first step

A 20-minute walkthrough of your enquiry path, then a two-week paid pilot on the front door.
