# Zero to one: action plan from the audits

Written 2026-11-14 from the 25 opportunity audits (15 passed independent checks) and the 26 website health checks (16 passed), on top of the first-customer plan dated 2026-11-12. "Zero to one" here means: first paying customer, then first monthly care fee. This is Claude's judgment from the audits; the response rates are unknown until the first ten conversations.

## What the audits change

1. **The front door comes before the agent, for everyone.** All 25 deep audits put a front-door repair first (a broken or unreadable website, an unclaimed Google profile, call links that fail, no quote or booking path), and the 26 health checks are businesses whose website is hacked, parked or gone. The agent is opportunity two in every audit. So the opener is not "do you want an AI receptionist" but "here is what your customers see today, dated and screenshotted".
2. **For the smallest shops, the agent alone does not pay for itself on time saved.** The modelled yearly value of an intake agent is $3,000–10,000 for most of the solo shops (a salon, a printer, a mechanic, a sole-practitioner lawyer), which is about the $5,400–7,800 a year the care fee costs. Where it clearly pencils: Artheon's office-engineer and pursuit agents ($45,000–95,000 and $30,000–65,000 a year), Distinct Engineering's bids and dispatch ($15,000–45,000 and $8,000–20,000), Lozito Law's closing coordination ($9,000–35,000), Federation Logistics' quote intake ($7,000–23,000), Midlantic's work orders and delinquencies ($5,000–17,000), Deborah Rose's bilingual intake ($9,000–23,000). All of these are modelled staff hours on stated assumptions, not cash, and must not be quoted as promises.
3. **Nine of the 25 have no named decision-maker anywhere public** (Yuhas, Kelly & Company, Hollander, Rakoty, Adway, Amertech, Distinct Engineering, the auto shop, B. Nathaniel). These need a visit or a phone call to find the owner; an email to nobody will not work.
4. **Some are off-limits or limited.** Molnar Roofing (a Chapter 7 filing by a company of that name, unconfirmed) and Ajulia Executive Search (a state stop-work order): do not contact until resolved. Bussel Realty: fine to contact, but only about the 404 contact page and missing enquiry form; the "hack" was our own browser's tab marker. Healthcare (First Rehab, Crystal Smiles, Rickards, Girgis, Hua Kang): a website fix handles no patient data and is fine; an intake agent is not, until a BAA is affordable. Artheon has 720 staff, well over the 200-staff cap used so far; keep it as one deliberate bigger bet or drop it.
5. **Timing matters for three of them.** Premier Properties' leasing waves are May–June and August–September, so a pilot has to start by March. Rakoty's audit and Form 990 season runs January to May, so December or January is the time to talk about document chasing. Roofers and paving contractors fix websites in winter.

## Steps, in order

**Step 0. Decide (by Friday 21 November).** Read the employment contract's outside-work, invention and non-solicit clauses and settle the exclusion list; this is still the first open question in the document and nothing else should start before it. Form the LLC ($100) and open the business account. Decide the Artheon size-cap question and confirm the front-door price. Pick a business name and register a domain.

**Step 1. Be real (by Friday 28 November).** A one-page site with the four-rung price card and the data-handling sheet. The pilot agreement from a lawyer or a SCORE mentor: liability capped at fees, no indirect damages, data addendum, client reviews outputs, inbound only; add a short fixed-price work order for front-door fixes. Request tech E&O and cyber quotes and ask in writing how AI errors are treated; insurance must be bound before the first paid job that touches a client's systems. Reformat the health checks and audits as printable leave-behinds without dollar figures. Set up the Retell demo line, labelled as a demonstration.

**Step 2. First ten conversations per opener (30 November to 19 December).** Front-door-led: Group A by walk-in (Milltown, East Brunswick, North Brunswick and South River are all within a few miles); the pitch is the dated screenshot, what a customer sees today, and the fixed price to fix it, with the agent mentioned only as "after the fix". Agent-led: Group B and the 11 November firms by email citing the audit's after-hours gap and giving the demo number; Group C by email to the named president. Five to ten contacts a week in total, split roughly evenly, two follow-ups at most, same-day replies, and ask every contact how they heard of us. One sheet for both: who, opener, what was shown, objection, next step.

**Step 3. First paid work (by 31 December).** Target two front-door fixes signed (fixed price, 50% deposit, one to two weeks each) and one agent pilot signed (paid, named owner, one success measure, end date). Whichever lands first is "one": the first invoice paid. Every fix ends with a one-page "what changed" with before-and-after screenshots, a request for a Google review and a reference, and a 15-minute conversation about what answers the phone after hours. Every pilot starts with the baseline count written down.

**Step 4. Second rung for each (January).** Fixed-front-door clients are offered the $1,200–1,500 pilot; pilot clients whose measure was met are offered setup and care with the front-door fix bundled in. Success measures come straight from the audits: after-hours calls answered and booked (Hughes, Miller, Yuhas, Biondo), misdials to zero (Yuhas), Sunday-to-Tuesday enquiries captured (Kelly & Company). One custom build at a time; fixes fill the gaps.

**Step 5. First care fee (February).** Convert a pilot to setup plus monthly care, three-month minimum, with the monthly review booked. Publish an anonymised case study with the client's agreement; ask for two referrals. Decide the NJSBA table. Then start the remaining Group C bets with the engineering firms as the second wave, and resume the backlog (health checks for the other 68 down sites; triage of the outer towns) only when the first-call list is exhausted.

## Weekly numbers

Per opener: businesses contacted, conversations held, first sales signed (fixes or pilots), second-rung sales, care fees active; plus referral partners signed. Review every Friday. At the end of January, keep the opener that produced paid work; if both did, the agent-led work gets the single build slot and front-door fixes fill around it. This replaces nothing in the three-track test; both openers feed Track A.

## Rules carried over

No promised savings; the audits' hour and dollar ranges are modelled on assumptions and stay out of the pitch. Inbound calls only. Never hold a client's passwords or registrar login; the client's own web person does account steps while we specify and verify. No work on a client system before insurance is bound and the agreement is signed. Prices marked "Checked" in the pricing section are safe to quote; the front-door price is an inference until NJSBDC or the first three jobs confirm it.
