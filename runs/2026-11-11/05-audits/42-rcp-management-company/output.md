# AI Opportunity Audit — RCP Management Company

You run roughly 125 associations and 15,000 doors across three New Jersey offices, and your own job posting shows how much of that still moves by hand: an onsite administrator answering every call, typing work orders, and back-charging owner accounts. That combination — real scale, real personal service, and a lot of manual re-keying — is exactly where a small, well-placed automation pays for itself.

## 1. A front desk that never misses a callback
**What's happening now:** Your contact page is a list of phone numbers and mailboxes, offices are open Monday–Friday 9–5, and new portal accounts wait up to two business days for staff review. Businesses like yours often find that the follow-up promise on the homepage becomes the first thing to slip when a manager is in board meetings.
**What the agent would do:** Read incoming email, web-form and transcribed phone inquiries, identify the community and unit, sort the request (maintenance, billing, violation, move-in, board matter), draft a reply, and open a callback task with a time window. Anything about money, fines or legal matters goes to a manager for approval first.
**Expected impact:** 15–30% less time spent handling inquiries on piloted communities — roughly 1.5–6 staff hours a week each, or $2.4k–$9.7k a year per community. Illustrative, based on published support-agent productivity research.
**Pilot:** 3–5 communities over 2–4 weeks, with a one-page response-time report you can show boards.

## 2. Work orders that enter themselves
**What's happening now:** Your Princeton administrator posting describes exactly this: building work orders from resident and manager requests, entering them, back-charging owner accounts, and keeping Buildinglink current.
**What the agent would do:** Pull unit, location, problem and urgency out of each request, suggest the trade and vendor, draft the work order and the back-charge with the rule cited, flag duplicates, and send the resident an acknowledgement. A manager approves every back-charge and fine before it posts.
**Expected impact:** 4–8 minutes saved per ticket; at an assumed 5,000–10,000 tickets a year, about 330–1,330 staff hours, or $8k–$32k annually. Illustrative.
**Pilot:** One region, 2–4 weeks, measured against a manually worked control group.

## 3. One place to ask "how does this community work?"
**What's happening now:** Two firms merged in 2023 into three offices, and public staff reviews mention inconsistent training and communication. Managers already spend their weeks on calls, email and meeting prep.
**What the agent would do:** Answer staff and board questions — bylaws, collection procedure, architectural requests, vendor terms — with the exact clause and page cited, scoped so one community's documents are never visible to another. Unanswered questions become a monthly list for your operations lead to fix.
**Expected impact:** 20–50% less time hunting for policy answers; conservatively $17k–$50k a year in recovered capacity, plus faster new-hire ramp. Illustrative.
**Pilot:** 3–4 weeks, 3–5 communities plus firm-wide procedures, checked against manager-written answers.

## Why this works
- **It runs itself, then stops.** Routine requests move without a person; anything touching money, fines or legal exposure waits for a manager's approval.
- **It fits what you already use.** Work lands in your existing portal and Buildinglink records rather than a new system staff have to learn.
- **Community data stays separate.** Each association's documents and records are walled off from the others, with a full record of who asked what.

## Suggested first step
A 20-minute walkthrough using one live community as the example, then a small paid pilot on 3–5 communities so the numbers are yours, not ours.
