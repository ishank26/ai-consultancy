# AI Opportunity Audit — Packaging & Distribution Resources (PDR)

You've grown from a few assembly lines in 2003 to 100+ clients, 20+ assembly lines and six Edison buildings covering 600,000+ sq ft, and in September 2026 you brought a 150,000 sq ft Toronto site live on the same order systems as Edison. What stands out is how much of that four-region network still runs through a shared inbox and a spreadsheet.

## 1. A faster front door for new client quotes

**What's happening now:** New business arrives as a downloaded Excel "New Customer Questionnaire" that prospects fill in offline and email to info@ for pricing. There's no form logic, quote calculator or client login on the site, so turnaround depends on who is at their desk.

**What the assistant would do:** Read each questionnaire and email, pull out the order profile (volumes, channels, kitting steps, ship-to regions), spot missing or contradictory answers, send one consolidated follow-up, and draft a complete quote packet from your approved rate cards. A person approves every number before it leaves.

**Expected impact:** 500–800 hours of quote preparation a year, roughly $12,000–$25,000, plus same-day first response. *(Inference: 15–40 requests a month at 2–6 hours each; volume isn't published.)*

**Pilot:** 2–4 weeks replacing the Excel round-trip on new inquiries, reporting median inquiry-to-quote-ready hours before and after.

## 2. Order exceptions across your 30+ sales channels

**What's happening now:** Orders flow from 30+ platforms through Order Desk into Dynamics, and your org chart carries that load with one EDI Analyst plus order processing and inventory roles. Toronto multiplies the exception surface without adding corporate headcount.

**What the assistant would do:** Watch incoming order events, classify each problem (bad address, duplicate, bundle mismatch, out-of-stock), propose a fix using that client's own rules, and either resolve the safe, reversible ones or hand a clerk a one-click approve/edit card. Every decision is logged per client.

**Expected impact:** 900–2,200 hours of clerk and analyst time, roughly $25,000–$60,000 a year, and fewer chargebacks from slow corrections. *(Inference: assumes 1.5–3 staff touch exceptions today.)*

**Pilot:** 10 days of read-only measurement on one channel, then three exception types behind a human approval queue.

## 3. One place to ask "where's this order?"

**What's happening now:** 100+ brand clients, several shifts and four regions reach you through one phone number and one generic inbox. Businesses like yours often find the answer lives in a particular person's memory of that client's SOP.

**What the assistant would do:** Answer staff questions from each client's own specs and routing guides plus live order status, always citing the source, always scoped to one client, escalating anything commercial to the account owner.

**Expected impact:** 500–1,850 hours of client-service capacity, $12,000–$40,000 a year; independent research on support assistants found 8–34% more issues resolved per hour, and we assume only 8–15%. *(Inference; other industry.)*

**Pilot:** Two clients with the richest spec binders, internal-only, measured on answer accuracy.

## Why this works

- **It proposes, your people approve.** Nothing prices, credits or ships without a person clicking yes.
- **It plugs into what you already run.** Dynamics, Order Desk and your channel integrations stay the system of record — no rip-and-replace, no new warehouse software.
- **Client separation is built in.** Each client's rules and documents stay in their own space, which matters with 100+ brand clients.

## Suggested first step

A 20-minute walkthrough of how the quote intake would work on one live questionnaire, then a small paid pilot on that single process.
