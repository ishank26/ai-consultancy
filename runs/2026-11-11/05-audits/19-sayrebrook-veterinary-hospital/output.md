# AI Opportunity Audit — Sayrebrook Veterinary Hospital

You are a seven-day, AAHA-accredited practice in Sayreville since 1980, advertising round-the-clock emergency care while the building is staffed 8am–7pm on weekdays. With about a dozen people covering clinic, boarding and housecalls — and every appointment still confirmed by a staff call-back — the front desk carries a lot that isn't clinical.

## 1. Someone always answers — day or night

**Now:** Requests come through a web form and wait for a staff call-back; same-day urgent slots depend on availability, with clients told to call early or ask about the cancellation list. Urgent calls, routine calls and callbacks all land on the same small team, seven days a week.

**What it would do:** Answer phone, texts and the form in English or Spanish, take pet details, and follow a red-flag symptom script your veterinarians sign off on. Real emergencies go to a person on call; everything else gets offered a slot staff confirm, plus reminders and automatic cancellation-list fills.

**Impact:** $7,000–$16,000 a year of staff time returned (exploratory estimate: 3–5 hours per open day, 30–40% resolved, New Jersey technician wages).

**Pilot:** 2–4 weeks, after-hours and overflow only, on one number plus the web form.

## 2. One inbox instead of six

**Now:** Intake arrives by phone, web form, a Gmail address, a new-client form, a patient-history form and some printable pages. Housecalls, pet transport and medical boarding have no booking mechanism beyond the general form or a call.

**What it would do:** Collect every submission into one review queue staff approve instead of retyping, flag incomplete forms, propose housecall routes and boarding placements that respect medication schedules, and draft payment follow-ups for staff to release.

**Impact:** $4,000–$11,500 a year of admin time, plus fewer dropped intake requests.

**Pilot:** 2–4 weeks on the web form and Gmail only, with the boarding board usable on day one.

## 3. Answers for a team that wears many hats

**Now:** Your employment page takes applications year-round and asks about holiday and extra-hours availability, and staff profiles show people moving from boarding to pharmacy to lab. Businesses like yours often find the same protocol questions get re-asked every few months.

**What it would do:** Answer staff questions from your own handbook and protocols, quoting the passage — boarding medication schedules, controlled-drug log rules, front-desk procedure — with a "not covered, ask the lead technician" fallback, and check drug-log entries for missing fields.

**Impact:** $1,500–$5,000 a year, with the larger value in faster ramp-up and audit-ready logs.

**Pilot:** 2–4 weeks, boarding and medical boarding only.

## Why this works

- **You keep approval:** no dosing advice, no record written, no billing message sent without a staff release.
- **It fronts the tools you use:** your phone, form, mailbox and vendor-hosted forms — nothing new for clients to learn.
- **Your data stays yours:** records and transcripts in an account you own, access by role, full audit trail.

## Suggested first step

A 20-minute walkthrough, no preparation needed. If it looks useful, a small paid pilot scoped to one opportunity; the figures above would be confirmed in a discovery session first.
