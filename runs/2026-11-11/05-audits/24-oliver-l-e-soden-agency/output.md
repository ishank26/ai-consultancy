# AI Opportunity Audit — Oliver L.E. Soden Agency

Family-owned since 1925, now three New Jersey offices and two brands, with a 5.0 average across roughly 177 Google reviews and a reply to about 97% of them. What stood out is that all of that service flows through one Account Management team in Jamesburg, covering Jamesburg, Little Silver and the Manasquan-based John B. Wright division.

## 1. A certificate and policy-change desk that drafts, you approve

**Now:** Your commercial lines postings describe the work plainly — process policy changes, issue certificates and evidence of insurance, re-verify contact details, document it in the agency system. The same role has been re-advertised across three towns at $30K–$85K, always requiring prior commercial lines experience.

**An AI assistant would:** read each request from the service inbox or CSR24, pull the policy and current forms, and draft standard certificates for one-click approval. Manuscript wording, new additional insureds or limits changes go to a person with a drafted carrier packet. It never binds, cancels or alters coverage.

**Impact:** $20,000–$50,000 a year of account management capacity (roughly 0.4–0.8 of a full-time role) — capacity, not a headcount cut.

**Pilot:** 3–4 weeks on the marine/marina certificates at John B. Wright, the most standardized book, measuring minutes per certificate against your baseline.

## 2. Someone answering the phone's questions after 5pm

**Now:** You advertise 24/7 phone and online service, while published hours are Monday–Friday 9–5 and postings advertise evenings off. Clients also have to pick the right portal from roughly 30 carrier claim and payment links.

**The assistant would:** on chat and email, identify the client and answer only from a curated list — which carrier portal handles billing, payments and claims, hours, directions, CSR24 steps — then link them there. Coverage questions go to a licensed staffer, matching the "cannot bind via chat" notice already on the John B. Wright site. Anything unanswered becomes a callback task with the transcript.

**Impact:** $12,000–$28,000 a year of service time deflected, plus after-hours enquiries captured that currently go nowhere.

**Pilot:** 2–4 weeks — build the carrier routing map and your top 40 questions, draft mode first, then live with handoff rules.

## 3. Commercial quote and renewal intake that stops the chasing

**Now:** Quoting starts from one embedded web form with no upload path, so loss runs, schedules and questionnaires get chased by email and phone, then re-keyed. Newer niche pages — contractor, marine, pickleball facility, nonprofit, cyber — multiply the questionnaires per submission.

**The assistant would:** send a secure link where clients upload applications, loss runs and schedules; extract the fields, check them against each carrier's requirements and email back a plain gap list. Renewals pre-fill at 120/90/60/30 days. An account manager reviews and binds every quote.

**Impact:** $8,000–$25,000 a year of account manager time, plus faster turnaround.

**Pilot:** 4 weeks, one line of business and one carrier set, tested against 20 historical submission packets.

## Why this works

- **It asks before it acts.** Certificates, quotes and coverage answers all wait for a named person's approval; nothing binds or changes coverage.
- **It fits your existing tools.** Work arrives where it already arrives — service inbox, CSR24, website form — and results land in your agency management system.
- **Client data stays client data.** Access controls, retained transcripts, and no training on client files.

## Suggested first step

A 20-minute walkthrough with Hal and whoever runs the certificate queue, using sample data. If it's useful, a small paid pilot on one book, scoped and priced before it starts.
