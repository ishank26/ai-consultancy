# AI Opportunity Audit — Fraser Brothers Group, LLC

You run three agency brands — Edison, Belford and Tinton Falls — with 17 named staff and about 40 carrier appointments, covering everything from personal auto to contractors, restaurants and Medicare. What stood out: every way a customer can start something runs through a phone call or a web form, and all three offices close between 4:15 and 5:00 PM on weekdays.

## 1. Every quote request gets an answer the same hour — even Sunday night

**What's happening now:** The site offers a quote form and a phone number — no online booking, no instant quote, no chat. Hours are weekday-only and differ by office, so a Friday-evening request waits until Monday, and someone sorts out by hand which of the three brands owns each lead.

**What the agent would do:** Read each form, voicemail and email, ask the three or four questions that matter, sort the request by line of business, suggest the right office and producer, offer a callback slot, and leave a drafted reply for a producer to approve.

**Expected impact:** roughly $20,000–$50,000 a year of intake and administrative time, using public New Jersey wage data. Faster first response is upside we haven't priced.

**Pilot:** four weeks, Edison office, personal auto and home only.

## 2. Certificates, endorsements and renewals stop sitting in an inbox

**What's happening now:** Seventeen people handle about 40 carrier markets, including niche programs like auto dealers, contractors and liquor liability. The only client self-service is a generic secure-upload link, so documents arrive without structure — and businesses like yours usually have someone opening, identifying and routing each one by hand.

**What the agent would do:** Recognize each file, pull the insured, policy numbers, limits and dates, draft the certificate or renewal packet, chase what's missing, and track the deadline. Anything uncertain goes to a person.

**Expected impact:** roughly $25,000–$60,000 a year of document-handling time; reduced rework isn't counted.

**Pilot:** four weeks, incoming certificate-of-insurance requests only, one office, checked against 100 documents.

## 3. One place to ask "does this policy cover it?" and "who do I call at the carrier?"

**What's happening now:** Staff compare coverage across many forms and about 40 carriers — you publish every carrier's claims number as a standing reference — and claims come in by phone during office hours. Coverage answers are also where mistakes cost the most.

**What the agent would do:** Search your own procedures, carrier guides and policy forms, then answer with the exact source, a confidence flag, the carrier contact, and a plain-English explanation staff can send to a client.

**Expected impact:** roughly $12,000–$35,000 a year of lookup and research time.

**Pilot:** three weeks, your top 10–15 carriers, staff only, tested on 150–200 real questions.

## Why this works

- **A licensed person approves everything client-facing** — no prices quoted, no coverage decisions issued, no certificate released unreviewed.
- **It plugs into what you already run:** the existing quote forms, the Bridge upload portal, your calendar and shared inbox.
- **Client information stays controlled:** role-based access, so benefits and life files are visible only to the right staff, with a record of who approved what.

## Suggested first step

A 20-minute walkthrough of the three ideas, then one small paid pilot — four weeks, one office, one workflow — so you see real numbers before deciding anything bigger.
