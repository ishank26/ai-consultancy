# AI Opportunity Audit — Benaka, Inc.

You have been building since 1994, mostly for federal clients — Air Force, Army and Navy design-build, JOC, IDIQ and maintenance work, plus an Air Force IDIQ at Joint Base McGuire-Dix-Lakehurst. Your QC Manager posting reads like a list of documents someone writes every day.

## 1. A second pair of hands for daily QC paperwork

**Now:** That posting lists three-phase control, preparatory meetings, minutes, RFIs, purchase orders and daily production reports, and asks for QCS/RMS familiarity alongside Word, Excel and Outlook; a related QC Manager/Superintendent listing advertises $100,000–$140,000.

**The agent:** Turns field notes and photos into draft daily reports, keeps a submittal register with overdue flags, drafts RFIs, minutes and three-phase checklists. Your QC manager signs.

**Impact:** $12,000–$30,000 per active project per year, about 6–10 drafting hours a week. **Pilot:** four weeks on one task order, 20 reports and RFIs against your QC manager's own.

## 2. A bid desk that never drops a Section L line

**Now:** You pursue an Air Force MACC, a Corps MATOC and JOC/IDIQ work; the Air Force solicitation behind the MACC drew 28 offers. Your project history and commendations live only as flat web pages, so from outside these write-ups look rebuilt by hand each time.

**The agent:** Watches SAM.gov and your vehicle portals daily, scores fit, produces a one-page bid/no-bid sheet, builds a compliance matrix from your three closest past-performance projects. Estimators keep pricing; you keep go/no-go.

**Impact:** $3,000–$10,000 a year in estimator time on today's rework share, more if assembly is drafted end to end. **Pilot:** one live pursuit, against your last comparable submission.

## 3. Subcontractor and certified-payroll tracking

**Now:** Subs email their details to the general inbox — no prequalification form, no vendor portal, just an address; the contact form has no qualification fields. NJ public work adds registration certificates and weekly certified payrolls filed through NJ Wage Hub.

**The agent:** Structured intake, certificate and expiry checks against the state's registered-contractor list, and a weekly payroll pack assembled and flagged before your staff files it. A person approves.

**Impact:** $6,000–$15,000 a year in clerical time plus avoided penalties — softest of the three, since the NJ-versus-federal split isn't public. **Pilot:** three weeks, then a month of payrolls alongside today's process.

## Why this works

- **It runs itself:** watches a mailbox or portal on a schedule.
- **It fits your tools:** drafts land in Word, Excel and Outlook; QCS/RMS untouched.
- **A person signs, and your data stays yours:** private environment, approval on every filing.

## Suggested first step

A 20-minute walkthrough on the QC example, then a small paid pilot on one task order.
