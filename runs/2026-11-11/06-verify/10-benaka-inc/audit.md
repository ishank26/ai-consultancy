# AI Opportunity Audit — Benaka, Inc.

You have been building for federal clients since 1994 — design-build, JOC, IDIQ and maintenance work for the Air Force, Army and Navy, plus an Air Force IDIQ at Joint Base McGuire-Dix-Lakehurst and a 2026 Army Corps small-business pool. What caught our attention is your own Quality Control Manager posting, which reads less like a job description than a list of documents someone has to write every day.

## 1. A second pair of hands for daily QC paperwork

**Now:** That posting bundles three-phase control forms, preparatory meetings, minutes, RFIs, purchase orders and daily production reports into one role, worked across QCS/RMS plus Word, Excel and Outlook — at $100,000–$140,000. Businesses like yours often find the drafting and reconciling takes the front and back of every day.

**The agent:** Turns field notes and photos into draft daily reports in your template, keeps a submittal register with overdue flags, drafts RFIs and minutes, pre-fills three-phase checklists. It drafts; your QC manager signs.

**Impact:** $12,000–$30,000 per active project per year (6–10 hours a week of drafting). **Pilot:** four weeks on one task order, with 20 reports and RFIs compared against your QC manager's own.

## 2. A bid desk that never drops a Section L line

**Now:** You pursue an Air Force MACC, a Corps MATOC and JOC/IDIQ work at once, and the Air Force solicitation behind the MACC drew 28 offers. Your project history and commendations exist only as flat web pages, so past-performance write-ups start from scratch.

**The agent:** Watches SAM.gov and your vehicle portals daily, scores fit, produces a one-page bid/no-bid sheet, builds a compliance matrix with your three closest past-performance projects. Estimators keep all pricing; you keep go/no-go.

**Impact:** $10,000–$30,000 a year in estimator time, plus room to pursue more with the same staff. **Pilot:** one live pursuit, measured against your last comparable submission.

## 3. Subcontractor and certified-payroll tracking

**Now:** Subs email their details to the general inbox — no form, no portal. On New Jersey public work you carry registration certificates and weekly certified payroll filings through NJ Wage Hub.

**The agent:** Structured prequalification intake, certificate and expiry checks against the state's registered-contractor list, and a weekly payroll pack assembled and flagged before your staff files it. Nothing goes out without a person approving.

**Impact:** $6,000–$15,000 a year in clerical time, plus avoided penalties — the softest estimate of the three, since the New Jersey-versus-federal split isn't public. **Pilot:** three weeks, then a month of payrolls run alongside your current process.

## Why this works

- **It runs on its own:** watches a project mailbox or portal on a schedule, no one has to remember.
- **It fits your tools:** drafts land in Word, Excel and Outlook; QCS/RMS stay untouched.
- **A person always signs:** every report, RFI and filing waits for your QC manager, PM or finance.
- **Your documents stay yours:** a private environment, not a consumer chatbot.

## Suggested first step

A 20-minute walkthrough on the QC example. If it lands, a small paid pilot on one task order.
