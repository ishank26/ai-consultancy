# AI Opportunity Audit — RAM Law

RAM Law has built a three-office plaintiff practice on being reachable: eleven certified civil trial attorneys, more than $170M recovered in ten years, and a bilingual site promising 24/7 phone intake. What stands out from outside is that this promise rests on people rather than systems — every inquiry arrives by phone or form, and the work behind each case is phone-and-document heavy.

## 1. After-hours inquiries get handled twice

**Now:** Intake is advertised 24/7, but offices run Monday–Friday 8am–8pm and close weekends, with no online scheduling or client portal. Every inquiry lands in one shared mailbox (contact@ram.law) for all three offices — and since cases are taken on contingency, a missed or unqualified first call is revenue that does not come back.

**The agent:** Answers calls and forms in English or Spanish, builds a structured intake record (incident date, county, treatment, insurance, callback window), flags time limits and duplicates, routes to the right office and books a specific callback. Injury calls escalate to on-call staff in seconds; every accept or decline stays a lawyer's decision.

**Impact:** roughly 12–30 staff hours a week back to case work, about $19,000–$47,000 a year at New Jersey support-staff wages, plus fewer inquiries waiting until 8am.

**Pilot:** 2–4 weeks on after-hours and overflow only, with a daily check that tracked calls became intakes.

## 2. Records and bills chased by hand, case by case

**Now:** The firm's own list of what it does for truck-crash clients includes organising medical bills, dealing with insurers, managing property-damage claims and finding treating specialists. The team is described as associates, counsel and paralegals, with no dedicated records, lien or billing role named — so that chase work sits with staff already supporting eleven trial attorneys.

**The agent:** Keeps a live checklist per case, drafts records requests and follow-ups from the file, sends them by the channel each provider actually uses, reads returned PDFs into a clean ledger (provider, date of service, billed, balance, lien), flags duplicates and gaps, and escalates stalled items to the responsible paralegal.

**Impact:** roughly 450–1,500 staff hours a year, about $15,000–$50,000 (plan on $20,000–$40,000), and a shorter records-to-demand timeline per case.

**Pilot:** 20–40 active truck or auto matters, one-click human release on every outbound letter.

## 3. Status questions answered in either language, at any hour

**Now:** Testimonials praise staff who return calls and keep clients updated, and the entire site — blog included — exists in Spanish. With no portal or case-status lookup, each "what's happening with my case" is a paralegal call during weekday hours, so bilingual service costs per call instead of scaling.

**The agent:** Answers only from the firm's own documents, procedures and case status fields — who is handling the case, whether records arrived, next appointment, how property damage and no-fault work — in English and Spanish, with a citation behind each answer and an immediate hand-off on anything about treatment, deadlines or money.

**Impact:** about 500–1,100 paralegal hours plus 2–4 attorney hours a week, roughly $15,000–$37,000 a year; plan at $15,000–$30,000.

**Pilot:** 25–50 matters, read-only, every reply reviewed before sending.

## Why this works

- **It runs itself, then stops.** Routine steps run unattended; a written rulebook escalates new injuries, deadlines, settlement figures, unhappy clients and low confidence.
- **It fits your existing tools.** Call tracking, the web forms and the shared inbox already produce the raw material — the agents sit around them, not in place of them.
- **People keep the decisions.** Case acceptance, fee terms, lien resolution and any message about treatment or damages require human approval, logged.
- **Data stays contained.** Privileged files and medical records mean a private, contracted setup with per-matter access limits and no training on your data — not an off-the-shelf chatbot.

## Suggested first step

A 20-minute walkthrough of how after-hours intake works today, then a small paid pilot on after-hours calls only — measured against your current baseline before anything daytime changes.
