# AI Opportunity Audit — Rich's Plumbing Heating & Air Conditioning, Inc.

Steady growth since 1989: three offices, a 4.7 Google rating and an advertised 60-minute emergency response (timing varies by conditions and location). Every path still ends at one number and a two-step callback form, with no time-slot picker online.

## 1. An assistant that answers the phone after hours

**What's happening now:** Three offices share one number, and staff must ask the safety questions your emergency page lists. A 60-minute promise is hard to hold overnight with a small team — is that where it bites?

**What the agent would do:** Answer overflow and after-hours calls, ask your safety questions in order, separate real emergencies from routine work, then book a slot or take a callback. It never quotes a price; hazards go to whichever technician is on call.

**Expected impact:** $20,000–$50,000 a year in intake hours avoided plus recovered after-hours bookings. *Illustrative.*

**Pilot:** 2–4 weeks, after-hours and overflow only.

## 2. Following up on estimates that go quiet

**What's happening now:** Estimates are given on site and explained in person, and as far as the public site shows, that's the only step. Reviews call your pricing fair but "a little high."

**What the agent would do:** When an estimate goes quiet it drafts a recap, a day-3 answer to the objection, a day-10 reminder and a day-21 close, using the coupons and financing you already offer. Staff approve every message.

**Expected impact:** $6,000–$28,000 a year in gross profit from reopened estimates. *Illustrative — the pilot counts stalled estimates.*

**Pilot:** 3 weeks approving follow-ups against the last 60 days of open estimates.

## 3. Keeping maintenance-plan records findable

**What's happening now:** Your plan promises check-ups, priority scheduling and records of equipment and service history. We can't tell from outside whether those records sit in software, spreadsheets or paper — businesses like yours often find only one person can find them.

**What the agent would do:** Put them in one place, then let staff and techs ask in plain words — a serial and warranty date, what was quoted last spring, who's due a tune-up. Every answer points to its record; customer messages are drafted for approval.

**Expected impact:** $6,000–$22,000 a year, mostly hours recovered, plus fewer lapsed renewals. *Illustrative.*

**Pilot:** 3 weeks on 150–250 plan customers, 30 records checked field by field.

## Why this works

- **Bounded, not autonomous.** Prices, diagnoses and warranty calls stay with your people.
- **Fits what you run.** Your lead form, consent box, financing links and coupon codes already exist.
- **Your data stays yours.** Records live in your systems, not a consumer chatbot.

## Suggested first step

A 20-minute walkthrough of the after-hours call flow, then a small paid pilot on evening and weekend calls.
