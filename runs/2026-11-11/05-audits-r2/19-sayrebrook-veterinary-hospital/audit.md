# AI Opportunity Audit — Sayrebrook Veterinary Hospital

You're a seven-day, AAHA-accredited practice in Sayreville, advertising 24-hour emergency care while posted hours run Mon–Thu 8–7, Fri–Sat 8–5, Sun 8–2, with technicians on site until at least 7pm seven days a week. LinkedIn lists 12 employees, and every appointment is still confirmed by a staff call-back.

## 1. Someone always answers

**Now:** Requests arrive by web form and wait for a staff call-back, and same-day slots are availability dependent; as far as your public pages show, urgent, routine and callback calls reach the same main number.

**What it would do:** Answer phone, texts and the form in English or Spanish, follow a red-flag symptom script your veterinarians approve, send emergencies to a person on call, and offer the rest a staff-confirmed slot with reminders and cancellation-list fills.

**Impact:** $7,000–$16,000 a year of staff time returned (exploratory: 3–5 hours per open day, 30–40% resolved, NJ technician wages).

**Pilot:** 2–4 weeks, after-hours and overflow only, on the main line and web form.

## 2. One inbox, not six

**Now:** Intake arrives by phone, web form, a Gmail address, three online forms and printable pages; housecalls and medical boarding have no booking route beyond the general form or phone.

**What it would do:** Gather every submission into one queue staff approve instead of retyping, flag incomplete forms, propose housecall routes and boarding placements around medication schedules, and draft payment follow-ups for staff to release.

**Impact:** $4,000–$11,500 a year of admin time, plus fewer dropped intake requests.

**Pilot:** 2–4 weeks on the web form and Gmail only, boarding board live on day one.

## 3. Answers for many hats

**Now:** Your employment page takes applications year-round and expects new hires to "learn the various protocols and adjust accordingly (after one or two weeks)"; staff profiles show one technician moving from boarding to pharmacy, another covering caretaking, assisting and lab work.

**What it would do:** Answer staff questions from your handbook and protocols, quoting the passage — boarding medication schedules, drug-log rules, front-desk procedure — with a "not covered, ask the lead technician" fallback, and flag missing fields in drug-log entries.

**Impact:** $1,500–$5,000 a year, with the larger value in faster ramp-up and audit-ready logs; the recurrence is an inference from your hiring page.

**Pilot:** 2–4 weeks, boarding and medical boarding only.

## Why this works

- **You keep approval:** no dosing advice, no record written, no billing message sent without a staff release.
- **It fronts your tools and keeps your data yours:** your phone, form, mailbox and vendor-hosted forms, records in an account you own, role-based access, full audit trail.

## Suggested first step

A 20-minute walkthrough, no preparation needed. If it looks useful, a small paid pilot on one opportunity; the figures above get confirmed in discovery first.
