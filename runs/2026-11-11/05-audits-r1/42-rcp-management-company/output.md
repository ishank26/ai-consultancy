# AI Opportunity Audit — RCP Management Company

At your 2023 merger announcement RCP reported 125 associations and 15,000 doors, and the combined firm now runs three offices. A posting for one of your Princeton onsite administrator roles lists the day-to-day: answering calls, typing work orders, back-charging owner accounts.

## 1. A front desk with a callback queue that closes
**Now:** Your contact page is a phone-and-email directory plus one web form, offices are open weekdays 9–5 per your public listing, and new portal accounts take two business days to approve. Reviews there complain about unreturned calls.
**The agent would:** Read email, web-form and transcribed calls, identify the community and unit, draft a reply and open a callback task. Anything about money, fines or legal matters waits for a manager.
**Impact:** 15–30% less inquiry-handling time on piloted communities — 1.5–6 staff hours a week, $2.4k–$9.7k a year, assuming 10–20 hours at $26/hour plus 20% overhead; the study behind the band brackets 14% and 34%. Illustrative.
**Pilot:** 3–5 communities, 2–4 weeks, one response-time report.

## 2. Work orders that enter themselves
**Now:** An RCP posting on a community-management job board describes it: building work orders from resident and manager requests, entering them, back-charging owners, updating Buildinglink.
**The agent would:** Pull unit, location, problem and urgency from each request, suggest trade and vendor, draft the work order and back-charge with the rule cited. A manager approves every back-charge first.
**Impact:** 4–8 minutes a ticket; at an assumed 5,000–10,000 tickets a year, 330–1,330 staff hours, or $8k–$32k. Illustrative.
**Pilot:** 2–4 weeks against a control group.

## 3. One place to ask how this community works
**Now:** Two firms merged in 2023 into three offices, which often means two sets of procedures and two document libraries.
**The agent would:** Answer staff and board questions on bylaws, collections, architectural requests and vendor terms, with the clause and page cited, scoped so one community's documents never reach another. Unanswered questions become a monthly fix-list.
**Impact:** 20–50% less time hunting for policy answers; assuming 20–30 managers and assistants each lose 1–2 hours a week (New Jersey manager median $39.83/hour), $17k–$50k a year after a 60% discount. Illustrative.
**Pilot:** 3–4 weeks, 3–5 communities, checked against manager-written answers.

## Why this works
- **It runs itself, then stops.** Routine requests move without a person; money, fines and legal matters wait for approval.
- **It fits your tools.** Work lands in your portal and Buildinglink records, not a new system.
- **Data stays separate.** Each association's documents are walled off, with a log.

## Suggested first step
A 20-minute walkthrough on one live community, then a small paid pilot.
