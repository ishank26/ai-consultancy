# AI Opportunity Audit — Rich's Plumbing Heating & Air Conditioning, Inc.

You've built something steady since 1989: three offices, a 4.7 Google rating in East Brunswick, and an advertised 60-minute emergency response. What stands out is that all of that demand still arrives through one phone number and a callback form — with no online booking anywhere on the site.

## 1. An assistant that answers the phone after hours

**What's happening now:** East Brunswick, Elizabeth and Bayville all publish the same number, and every page sends customers to your office staff. Your emergency page tells callers to phone in, and staff are expected to ask the safety questions it lists — where the leak is, whether water is still flowing, whether the shutoff is closed, whether there's an electrical hazard — before anyone rolls a truck. Businesses like yours often find that same 60-minute promise strains a small office team overnight.

**What the agent would do:** Answer the line on overflow and after hours, ask your own safety questions in order, sort true emergencies from same-day and routine work, then book a slot or promise a callback window. It texts confirmation and a "tech on the way" update. It never quotes a price, and anything hazardous is transferred straight to your on-call technician.

**Expected impact:** $20,000–$50,000 a year in intake hours avoided plus recovered after-hours bookings. *Illustrative.*

**Pilot:** 2–4 weeks, after-hours and overflow only, with no change to your phone menu.

## 2. Following up on estimates that go quiet

**What's happening now:** Estimates are given on site and explained in person — and for most jobs that appears to be the only conversation. Reviews call your pricing fair but "a little high," which is exactly the objection a quiet second touch answers. You already offer Synchrony financing and six coupons (RICH50, BOILER500, HVAC300); reaching a stalled estimate with them is a manual step today.

**What the agent would do:** When an estimate is declined or deferred, it drafts a recap with the numbers, a day-3 answer to the specific concern, a day-10 reminder and a day-21 close — then routes any "ready to book" reply to your office as a callback task. Staff approve every message before it sends.

**Expected impact:** $6,000–$28,000 a year in gross profit from reopened estimates. *Illustrative — the pilot measures how many stalled estimates actually exist.*

**Pilot:** 3 weeks drafting and approving follow-ups against the last 60 days of open estimates.

## 3. Keeping maintenance-plan records findable

**What's happening now:** Your plan sells peace of mind — seasonal check-ups, priority scheduling, and records of equipment, serial numbers and service history. Long-tenure customers in your reviews mean those histories run years deep, and right now that knowledge lives with whoever can find it.

**What the agent would do:** Put those records in one place, then let staff and techs ask in plain language — a boiler's serial and warranty date, what was quoted last spring, which plan customers are due a heating tune-up. Every answer points to the record it came from, and any customer message is drafted for staff approval.

**Expected impact:** $6,000–$22,000 a year, mostly hours recovered plus fewer lapsed renewals. *Illustrative.*

**Pilot:** 3 weeks on 150–250 plan customers, with 30 records checked field by field.

## Why this works

- **Bounded, not autonomous.** The agent handles the repetitive part and hands anything uncertain to your team — prices, gas or electrical diagnoses, and warranty decisions stay with people.
- **It fits what you already run.** Your lead form, SMS consent checkbox, Synchrony links and coupon codes are already in place; nothing gets replaced in week one.
- **Your customers' details stay yours.** Records and transcripts live in your own systems, not a consumer chatbot.

## Suggested first step

A 20-minute walkthrough of the after-hours call flow, then a small paid pilot on evening and weekend calls only — measured, with an exit either way.
